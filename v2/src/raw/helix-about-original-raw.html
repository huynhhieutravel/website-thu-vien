Volume I — On Looking

The archive is a coil,not a shelf.

Helix keeps thirty prints in permanent rotation. Nothing is filed and nothing is retired: the strand turns, a frame arrives at the centre, and for as long as you hold it there it is the only picture in the room.

Everything else is deliberately out of focus. That is not a flourish, it is the argument.

It began as a shoebox that nobody could keep shut.

Four photographers, one box of prints, and an argument that ran for most of a winter: which frames deserved the wall. We never settled it. Instead we hung them on a wire and let the wire turn.

The site you are standing in is that wire, wound tighter. Thirty frames, two turns, no end and no beginning worth naming.

A photograph filed away is a photograph that has stopped working.

From the first wall text, 2019

Working notes

01

Rotation over ranking

There is no best frame and no front page. The strand turns at the same patient rate for all thirty prints, so the picture you meet first is an accident of timing rather than a verdict.

02

One picture at a time

Everything you are not looking at softens, dims and falls back into the fog. Attention is the whole medium here; the coil simply makes it visible.

03

Prints, not files

Each frame is scanned from a physical print, grain and all. What you see on the strand carries the paper it was made on, not the sensor it came from.

04

The caption comes last

A photograph should hold you before it tells you where it was taken. Titles, cities and film stock arrive only once the frame has settled at the centre.

The strand is always turning. Send us something worth putting on it.
